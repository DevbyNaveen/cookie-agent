Dictation capture and final-result audit — 2026-10-31

Evidence
The screenshot's No speech heard message was not reliable evidence of quiet speech.
App logs showed final transcripts of 44 and 11 characters reaching the controller
approximately 0.337 and 0.132 seconds after key release. Earlier attempts included
zero delivered audio and successful 24/51-character helper results.

Confirmed software defects and changes
1. DictationController's four-second timeout used try? sleep. Cancelling it when a
   final arrived woke it immediately; with no partial/live text, it invalidated the
   interaction and cancelled the successful insertion. Replaced with a cancellation-
   aware safety deadline. Provider deadlines own recognition; absent partials no
   longer mean silence.
2. Microphone-button sessions never submitted their final text for insertion.
   Empty-draft button sessions now submit just like keyboard sessions.
3. Empty finals and provider errors now reach the bar explicitly. No captured audio,
   exactly silent input, recognition failure and processing timeout are distinguished.
4. Late partials cannot overwrite a confirmed final. Old deadline callbacks check
   the recording identity. Releasing while preparing reports that state immediately.
5. Capture failure releases the speech session. Each attempt opens a fresh audio
   engine against the current input route. A two-second no-buffer watchdog detects
   an engine that started but never delivered microphone data.
6. The tap captures its own transcription session instead of reading mutable
   main-actor state. UI level updates check the session identity.
7. Tap samples are copied before the engine reuses them. Resampling and helper pipe
   writes run on a serial queue off the audio callback. Finish is enqueued after
   accepted audio. Conversion failures fail closed instead of silently losing audio.
8. Added aggregate frame/RMS/peak diagnostics, without saving recordings or transcript
   contents. Recognition audio has no new gate, threshold, gain or normalization.

Follow-up: startup responsiveness
User confirmed dictation now works but described an uncomfortable first-second delay.
Inspection found AVAudioEngine input setup/start on the main actor, after awaiting
recognizer initialization. The engine now starts on a dedicated serial capture queue;
stop is serialized on that queue too. Microphone capture begins before recognizer
session setup, with up to six seconds of owned audio buffered until the session
attaches. Overflow fails explicitly instead of truncating the beginning. Release
during recognizer setup drains that buffer and sends finish in order. Cancellation
discards the buffer. There is no recording before the user's dictation action.

The controller publishes microphone readiness from actual samples; home, status,
onboarding and floating bar say Starting microphone until then. Audio levels can
animate while the recognizer opens. Startup diagnostics report time to first audio
and recognizer attachment separately. The 200ms Ctrl+Cmd shortcut arming guard
remains to protect other keyboard shortcuts.
